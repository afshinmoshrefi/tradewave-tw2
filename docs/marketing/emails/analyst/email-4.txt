How to Read the Evidence, So the Breadth Actually Pays Off

Total-market breadth is only worth $47/mo if you can tell a real edge from a flattering one. That skill is the whole game. Here is the floor, in four moves.

1. Read Win Rate With Sharpe and TWR, Never Alone
Direction is not enough. Win rate ("won 19 of the last 20 years") tells you direction. Sharpe tells you whether the path was smooth or a white-knuckle ride. Alongside Sharpe, watch TWR, the TradeWave Ratio - a Sharpe-like score that also credits how far a window ran in your favor (its maximum favorable excursion, defined below), so it rewards windows that moved strongly your way along the path even when they did not finish at the peak. High win rate with a strong Sharpe and TWR is a clean, repeatable drift. High win rate with weak ones is lumpy - tradeable, but it calls for staged or hedged tactics. The pairing is the single most important read on the screen.

2. Check the Median Against the Average (the Hero-Year Test)
One year can fake an edge. An average can be carried by a single monster year. If the median gain is far below the average, one "hero year" is doing the lifting and the typical year looks nothing like the headline. The bar chart shows you instantly: is the green broad-based, or is it one tall bar and a crowd of small ones?

3. Respect Sample Size
A few years is not a record. A 100% win rate on 2 occurrences is not an edge, it is a coincidence with good marketing. We show it and we flag it. High percentages only mean something on a meaningful number of years. If you are unsure, ask Tara to pressure-test it: "is this sample size meaningful, and is the median tracking the average?" Tara only reads the stats the engine already produced, so its answer is grounded in your pattern, never invented.

4. Set the Exit Before You Ever Act
Decide your lines first. MFE (maximum favorable excursion) is the best the pattern has historically reached - use it to set a target. MAE (maximum adverse excursion) is the worst heat it put you through - use it to set a stop and your size. Seasonality is probability, not protection - a 20-year winner can still take a hard adverse move in any single year. Decide the target and the line that says "this is wrong" before you act, not after.

Then organize it: when a pattern survives all four checks, save it to one of your 25 portfolios and set a calendar reminder (the Portfolio Manager fires it when the window opens) so the setup comes back to you on its own.
