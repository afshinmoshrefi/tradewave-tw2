Validate Evidence Across the Full U.S. Market

Analyst’s U.S. stock-and-ETF breadth becomes useful when you can separate durable evidence from a flattering result.

Here is the minimum validation process.

1. Read Win Rate With Sharpe And Twr

Win rate tells you how often the pattern finished profitably.

Sharpe helps describe how smooth or volatile the historical path was.

TWR, the TradeWave Ratio, is a Sharpe-like measure that also accounts for maximum favorable excursion — how far the window historically moved in its favorable direction along the way.

A high win rate supported by stronger Sharpe and TWR values is more convincing than a high win rate with a weak or unstable path. None of these metrics should be used alone.

2. Check The Median Against The Average

One unusually strong year can pull an average upward.

If the median gain sits far below the average, a “hero year” may be doing too much of the work. Inspect the yearly bars and ask whether the result is broad-based or dominated by one outlier.

3. Respect Sample Size

A 100% result across two occurrences is not the same evidence as a strong result across decades.

High percentages become more informative when they survive a meaningful sample. If you are unsure, ask Tara whether the sample is thin and whether the median supports the average. Tara reads the statistics produced by the engine rather than inventing new figures.

4. Examine Mfe And Mae

Maximum favorable excursion shows how far a pattern historically moved in the favorable direction.

Maximum adverse excursion shows how far it historically moved against the pattern.

Use them to understand the historical path and risk. Any decision about targets, stops, position size, or whether to trade remains entirely yours.
