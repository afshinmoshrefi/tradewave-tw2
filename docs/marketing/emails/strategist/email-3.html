TRADEWAVE STRATEGIST

A Pattern Is Not a Buy Signal. It Is Evidence to Read

The Opportunity Table ranks candidates. Ranked highly is not the same as approved. Every candidate earns its place only after you validate it in the Wave Viewer.

The three views, in this order

1. Profit Bar Chart - one bar per year, with the losing years left in. Is the edge broad-based or carried by one exceptional year?

2. Trend Chart - the typical historical path. Where is the recurring pivot, and where is the weak stretch?

3. Stats Table - confirm the record with % Profitable, Sharpe, median versus average, and cumulative return versus buy and hold.

The evidence is strongest when all three views tell a consistent story.

The One Judgment That Separates Edge From Noise

Read win rate with Sharpe. A high hit rate describes direction. Sharpe adds information about how smooth or volatile the historical path was. Never trust a high percentage without checking sample size: 91% across 11 occurrences is more fragile than the headline makes it sound.

Then check the path itself. MFE and MAE describe the best favorable and worst adverse excursions along the historical paths. They add context about variability and path risk; they are not automatic targets or stops. TWR adds information about how strongly a window moved along the way.

Seasonality is probability, not protection. Treat these statistics as research context, not trading instructions.

Where the Cycle Lens Earns Its Keep

The cycle appears in two places: the overlay on a loaded pattern and the election-cycle filter on the Opportunity Table.

Compare the full consecutive-year record with the matching PE-cycle subset. The cycle sample is usually smaller, so treat a higher percentage with extra skepticism rather than assuming it is automatically stronger.

Use the same comparison across the full market set. If the consecutive and cycle records disagree, investigate the difference instead of choosing the more attractive headline.
