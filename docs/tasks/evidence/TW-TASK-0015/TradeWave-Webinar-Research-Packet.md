**Ten proposed talking points**

1. **“The window has opened. The result has not been written.”** State the September 28 measured starting close and July 19, 2027 final measurement date. Lock the rule before discussing a preferred outcome. Show that 2026 is absent from the completed history.

2. **“Twenty-three green bars can hide a frightening ride.”** Reveal the 2018 bar first, then its -19.47% MAE. Its +2.78% finish did not make the experience easy. Ask attendees what additional evidence they would need before treating a win rate as useful.

3. **“A century of history gives us 24 observations here.”** Explain PE+2 and the four-year spacing. Ten midterm occurrences are a different dataset from ten consecutive years. The audience should leave able to read that distinction on any chart.

4. **“The losing year belongs at the center of the evidence.”** Show 1930 immediately, with its -23.41% finish and -35.38% MAE. Describe what would weaken the thesis now, without promising that a single failure identifies a new Depression.

5. **“A 0.03% win is still almost nothing before costs.”** Use 1978 to show why the count of winners, the size of results and the route taken must be read together.

6. **“Headlines and the calendar answer different questions.”** TradeWave supplies seasonal context. The macro section supplies current growth, inflation, rates and earnings evidence after independent verification. Anne-Marie can add her current-price and risk framework. Agreement is useful context; it is not an automatic buy signal.

7. **“The short-term scan and the nine-month index study are different studies.”** Explain that the September 16 94% figure describes qualifying short patterns in a filtered result set. It is neither a 94% crash probability nor a claim that 94% of the index's constituents will fall.

8. **“A perfect record gets more interesting when we try to break it.”** Load AME's ten midterm cycles, then keep the dates fixed and switch to ten consecutive years. Show the 2021 loss and the 2019 entry-year path through a severe decline before a positive finish. Keep both results visible.

9. **“A useful research tool must be allowed to say no.”** Optional third demo: AAPL's fixed September long study returns four wins, six losses, -1.82% average and a negative median. Do not force every familiar ticker into the bullish narrative.

10. **“Repeat the question on your own shortlist.”** The reusable job is to discover a candidate, inspect every year, test another sample, combine independent current evidence, then save the exact research configuration. Invite a trial on one real research question, with the same willingness to reject an idea.

**Workflow and product positioning**

TradeWave's core job is seasonal research: the same instrument, direction and calendar window over a chosen historical cohort. It can begin with its Opportunity Table to discover candidates, or with an investor's familiar symbol and question.

The documented loop is scan, validate, organize and act on one's own decision. Validation reads per-year outcome bars, the historical Trend Chart and summary statistics together. Preserve the exact symbol, direction, dates, duration and year selection when saving or sharing a study. Changing a date or excluding years creates a new study and must remain visibly labeled.

The current production describe_tradewave response explicitly separates seasonal information from fundamentals, valuation, news, macro/rates and catalysts. Do not describe the seasonal engine as doing the webinar's economic research. Some public website feature lists mention ancillary news, estimates and real-time information; their presence does not turn the engine results into a fundamental investment conclusion.

The Trend Chart is a normalized 0–100 seasonal index, not dollars, a forecast return, or separate cumulative paths for every historical year. MFE/MAE describe extremes inside the selected window; neither is an achievable-return promise. Keep proprietary TWA/TWR to a brief optional explanation if asked. The guest audience can grasp count, average, median, losing years and path risk before learning extra metrics.

Distinguish three measures: historical seasonal frequency; a separately labeled model probability when actually returned; and the forward daily-pick ledger with its own target-hit/close scoring rules. A forward ledger for selected daily picks does not validate every historical calendar window. This packet does not audit that ledger or sell its performance.

**Objections worth inviting**

| Audience objection | Honest response |
|---|---|
| “Did you search until you found dates that worked?” | Exact dates were selected using these same completed cycles. That creates selection risk. The full rows and rule are public, and Observation 25 provides new evidence under the rule. One new result will not remove the original selection problem. |
| “Does 23 out of 24 mean a 96% chance now?” | It is a historical share in a small, selected sample. It is not a calibrated probability for this cycle. |
| “Why should election years matter?” | They define a repeatable comparison cohort. Calendar-related policy and investor behavior are possible explanations, but the chart does not establish causation or that the president controls returns. |
| “Why exactly September 27?” | The public record reports the selected fixed date pair. The precision makes the study reproducible; it does not establish that one day is a uniquely knowable future bottom. |
| “Is shifting the entry to a better-looking pivot more scientific?” | It produces a different study. Show the original alongside it. Even a small adjustment made after seeing returns adds selection risk; calling it alignment does not remove that risk. |
| “Why not delete the outlier years?” | Inspect them, verify their data if needed, and label a separate comparison. Do not silently remove inconvenient observations or present the cleaner subset as the original sample. The Hubbell video is useful for teaching this limitation. |
| “Does -20% mean I should use that stop?” | No. It is Afshin's historical reference from the entry close. It is not an optimized risk rule, and future losses can exceed any historical bound. |
| “Has this survived every crash?” | The fixed windows did not contain every famous crash. Do not claim they included the October 1987 crash, the 2008 collapse or the early-2020 pandemic crash merely because the historical span covers those dates. |
