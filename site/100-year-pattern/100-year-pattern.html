The rule, stated before the result

Calendar dates first. Trading dates second.

01

Enter

September 27 of a midterm election year.

02

Hold

295 calendar days, counted inclusively. The entry date is day 1.

03

Exit

July 18 of the following year.

04

Resolve

If a date is not a trading day, use the first trading day after it.

Counting example: July 1 through July 31 is 31 days. This is TradeWave's general calendar convention - not special handling created for this pattern.

Methodology and limits

What this demonstrates - and what it does not.

TradeWave methodology →
01

Calculation

Returns are SPX price returns calculated close-to-close from the resolved entry and exit shown in the CSV. No transaction costs or slippage are included.

02

Excursions

The chart reproduces TradeWave's displayed MFE and MAE. Early-history MAE is close-based; later history uses intraday data where available. The CSV retains the separate fields.

03

Sample status

All 24 completed cycles are in-sample. They demonstrate repetition; they do not prove a mathematical law. The cycle from 2026 to 2027 is the first prospective out-of-sample test and is excluded from every historical statistic until it resolves.

04

Benchmark context

The 118% buy-and-hold figure shown in the book and TradeWave export uses Jan 1 to Jan 1 annual periods. It is not a like-for-like Sep to Jul benchmark, so it is not plotted here.
