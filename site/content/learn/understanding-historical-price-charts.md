Can historical stock performance forecast future prices? **Not reliably as an exact price forecast.** Historical price data can reveal recurring average seasonal tendencies, but future prices are also affected by new information, market conditions, and events that are not contained in the historical record.

TradeWave separates historical tendency from prediction by showing actual year-by-year price behavior alongside 60-calendar-day seasonal projections anchored to the latest actual price. These projections describe average historical paths; they are not forecasts, price targets, or guarantees.

## Key Takeaways
- TradeWave Historical Price Charts show how a security performed during a seasonal window in a specific year.
- The Current Price Chart can display two 60-calendar-day seasonal projections anchored to the latest actual price.
- **Proj** uses the number of years selected in the Years Filter.
- **Proj N-Y** uses every year of price history available for that security; the value of N changes by security.
- Each projection line has its own toggle, so traders can display either line or compare both.
- Seasonal projections are average historical trend paths, not forecasts or price targets.

## What Are Historical Price Charts in TradeWave?

TradeWave Historical Price Charts allow traders to examine how a security actually performed during a specific year within a selected seasonal trading wave.

Instead of viewing only aggregated historical averages, traders can drill down into individual years to better understand how the market behaved under varying economic conditions, volatility regimes, and macro environments.

This deeper level of analysis helps traders identify both consistency and exceptions within seasonal patterns.

## How to Access Historical Price Charts

Click any bar in the bar chart to display the Historical Price Chart for that specific year.

For example, selecting the 2022 bar loads the security's historical price behavior for that occurrence. TradeWave automatically switches to the corresponding chart view.

You can also use the navigation dots on the horizontal blue ribbon to move between the Historical Price Chart, Current Price Chart, and other supporting views.

## How to Analyze Historical Trading Behavior

### Perform Year-by-Year Analysis

Each selected year provides a detailed snapshot of how the security behaved during that historical period.

This allows traders to evaluate how the same seasonal wave performed during different market environments.

### Identify Seasonal Consistency

Comparing multiple years helps reveal recurring seasonal trends, recurring strengths or weaknesses, and periods where patterns broke down.

### Study Market Context

Historical Price Charts can also provide insight into how macroeconomic events, earnings cycles, interest rate changes, or broader market conditions influenced performance.

## What Is the TradeWave Current Price Chart?

The TradeWave Current Price Chart combines recent actual price history with optional seasonal projection lines. These projections begin at the latest actual price and extend 60 calendar days into the future, making it possible to compare the current price path with the average seasonal trend from two historical samples.

## How Do TradeWave Seasonal Projections Work?

A TradeWave seasonal projection is the average trend path produced by the analyzed years. It is anchored to the security's latest actual price so that the historical average path can be viewed in current-price terms.

The projection describes the average direction and shape of the historical path over the next 60 calendar days. It is not a forecast, a price target, or a promise that the security will follow the same route.

### Proj: Projection Based on the Selected Years

The **Proj** toggle displays the seasonal projection calculated from the number of years currently selected in the Years Filter.

In the CB example, the Years Filter is set to 20 years. The gold projection line therefore represents the average 60-calendar-day trend path across that selected 20-year sample.

### Proj N-Y: Projection Based on All Available Years

The **Proj N-Y** toggle displays a second seasonal projection calculated from every year of historical price data available for the selected security.

The number in the label changes by security. CB has 32 available years in this example, so the interface displays **Proj 32-Y** and plots the corresponding purple projection line. A security with a different amount of available history will show a different value of N.

### Compare Either Projection or Both

The two projection controls are independent toggles. Traders can turn either projection on or off, or display both lines at the same time.

Comparing both lines shows whether the average seasonal path changes when the analysis expands from the selected lookback to the security's full available history. Similar paths indicate that both samples produced a similar average shape. Visible differences show where the selected-years result and the all-history result diverge.

## How Should Seasonal Price Projections Be Interpreted?

Use the projection lines to study historical tendency, not to predict an exact future price.

- Focus on the direction, timing, and shape of each average path rather than treating projected values as targets.
- Compare the selected-years projection with the all-history projection to see how sensitive the average path is to the chosen lookback.
- Review individual Historical Price Charts to see the real year-by-year outcomes behind the averages.
- Combine the projections with TradeWave statistics, trend analysis, and current market context before making a trading decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can Stock Prices Be Forecast From Historical Performance?

Historical performance cannot reliably forecast an exact future stock price. It can reveal average seasonal tendencies that occurred across the analyzed years. TradeWave visualizes those historical tendencies from the latest actual price so traders can compare the selected lookback with all available history. The resulting paths are research tools, not forecasts or price targets.

### Are TradeWave seasonal projections forecasts?

No. They are average historical trend paths anchored to the latest actual price. They do not forecast an exact future price or guarantee that a seasonal pattern will repeat.

### How far forward do the seasonal projections extend?

Both projection lines extend 60 calendar days from the latest actual price.

### Why does the all-history projection show a different number of years for different securities?

Each security has a different amount of available price history. The **Proj N-Y** label automatically shows the total number of years available for the selected security.

### Can both seasonal projection lines be displayed together?

Yes. **Proj** and **Proj N-Y** are independent toggles, so either line or both lines can be displayed.

## Why Historical Price Charts and Seasonal Projections Matter

Seasonal analysis becomes significantly more useful when traders can move beyond a single average and evaluate both the individual historical outcomes and the effect of changing the lookback period.

Historical Price Charts reveal the individual outcomes behind a seasonal pattern. The Current Price Chart then translates the selected-years and all-history averages into two comparable 60-calendar-day seasonal paths. Together, these views provide both the detailed historical record and the current-price context needed for more informed seasonal research.

This distinction is consistent with [Investor.gov guidance on investment performance claims](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-47): historical performance can provide useful context, but it cannot predict how an investment will perform in the future.
