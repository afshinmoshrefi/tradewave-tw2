## Key Takeaways

- TradeWave follows one loop: **Scan, Validate, Organize, Act**.
- The Opportunities Table is **triage**. Ranked highly does not mean approved.
- Validation happens in the Wave Viewer in a fixed order: **Profit Bar Chart, Trend Chart, Stats Table**. Act only when all three agree.
- The Portfolio Manager saves the full configuration of each pattern, not a picture of it, so you can recall it, track it and set reminders.
- Acting means deciding the target, the stop and the time horizon **before** you enter.

## 1. Scan: Find Candidates

Open the [Opportunities Table](/learn/finding-high-probability-trading-waves.html), choose a universe of securities and a start date, and let it rank the windows with the strongest historical record. Before you read a single row, set the two controls that define the data:

- **The lens:** consecutive years, or one phase of the presidential election cycle. See [the Presidential Election Cycle Lens](/learn/presidential-election-cycle-regimes.html).
- **The depth:** how many years are in the sample. See [How Many Years of Data Do You Need?](/learn/how-many-years-of-data-seasonal-backtest.html)

Then shortlist three to five candidates. Do not treat the list as a buy list. Its job is to save you from searching thousands of windows by hand.

## 2. Validate: Read the Evidence in a Fixed Order

Click a candidate to open it in the [Wave Viewer](/learn/using-the-wave-viewer.html) and read the three views in this order:

1. **Profit Bar Chart.** One bar per year. Is the record broad-based, or is one huge year carrying the average? Were the losing years small?
2. **Trend Chart.** The many years compressed into the most typical path. Is the direction coherent? Where does the move usually begin (the pivot zone) and where does it usually stall? See [Understanding the Trend Chart](/learn/understanding-the-trend-chart.html).
3. **Stats Table.** The numbers that confirm or contradict what you saw: percent profitable, [Sharpe Ratio](/learn/sharpe-ratio-explained.html), average and median gain, standard deviation and cumulative return against buy and hold. See [Understanding the Stats Table](/learn/understanding-the-stats-table.html).

If the three views disagree, pass. There will be another candidate tomorrow.

## 3. Organize: Save What Passes

Save each validated pattern to the [Portfolio Manager](/learn/using-the-portfolio-manager.html). A saved pattern stores the whole configuration: entry and exit dates, direction, days, the year set, group and notes. That makes it reproducible. Next year you can recall the identical analysis instead of trying to remember which settings you used.

Use the notes field as a trading journal: why you liked it, what you planned, what happened.

## 4. Act: Plan the Exit Before the Entry

Every position needs three numbers decided in advance:

- **A target**, informed by how far the window typically ran in your favor ([MFE](/learn/mfe-and-mae-explained.html)).
- **A stop**, informed by how far it typically went against you (MAE), sized so a stop-out costs a small, fixed share of the account. See [Position Sizing and Stop Losses](/learn/position-sizing-and-stop-loss-seasonal-trades.html).
- **A time horizon**, the window's end date. Holding past the typical peak gives gains back. See [When to Exit a Seasonal Trade](/learn/when-to-exit-a-seasonal-trade.html).

Seasonality sets the bias, not the trigger. Many traders also confirm the entry with current price action, such as the price holding above its 50-day moving average, and pass or cut size when the chart strongly disagrees with the calendar.

Finally, set the calendar reminders. Seasonal edges are usually lost in execution, not in research.

## A Daily and Weekly Rhythm

- **Active traders:** a short daily scan for new windows opening in the next few days, and a longer weekly session to validate and plan.
- **Long-term investors:** check positions monthly, not daily, and revisit windows once a year. See [Seasonal Investing for Long-Term Investors](/learn/seasonal-investing-for-long-term-investors.html).

Pick one style and commit to it for a few months. Consistency beats the best-looking average.
