## Key Takeaways

- Long-term investors use seasonality to answer **when**, not **what**: when a holding has historically been weak, and when it has done most of its work.
- The benchmark is always **buy and hold**. A seasonal window only earns its place if its cumulative return beats simply owning the security all year.
- Long seasonal holds typically run **150 to 340 days** and are measured over **25 years or more**.
- The broad market has historically done most of its compounding from late October to early September, and stumbled in a weak stretch TradeWave calls **the Lost Summer** (about September 4 to October 25).
- Moving in and out has **tax and cost consequences**. Many investors use seasonality to time additions, trims and hedges rather than full exits.

## Investors and Traders Use Seasonality Differently

| | Active trader | Long-term investor |
|---|---|---|
| Holding period | 10 to 120 days | 150 to 340 days, or the whole year |
| Main question | Is this short window reliable? | Does avoiding the weak stretch beat holding all year? |
| Key metrics | Win rate, Sharpe Ratio, MFE, MAE | Cumulative return vs buy and hold |
| Review cadence | Daily or weekly | Monthly, with an annual reset |

## The Long-Term Workflow

### 1. Start With Buy and Hold

In the Wave Viewer, look at the full calendar year first. Buy and hold in TradeWave means owning from January 1 to January 1, repeated across every year in the sample. This is the bar every seasonal idea must clear.

### 2. Find the Weak Stretch

Open the [Trend Chart](/learn/understanding-the-trend-chart.html). It compresses many years into the most typical path through the calendar. Look for a recurring dip: a stretch where the line usually falls or stalls.

### 3. Flip to the Strong Window

The Reverse Date Range tool flips a window to its complement. If the weak stretch is September 5 to October 3, the reverse is October 3 to September 5 of the next year, the part of the year you would actually hold. Compare its cumulative return with buy and hold.

### 4. Keep It Only if It Beats Buy and Hold

If the seasonal hold window's cumulative return is not clearly higher than buy and hold, the timing is not adding value. Keep holding all year.

### 5. Save It and Check Monthly

Save the configuration to the [Portfolio Manager](/learn/using-the-portfolio-manager.html), track it monthly and revisit it once a year. Long windows do not need daily attention.

## What the Evidence Shows

Across S&P 500 history from 1928 to 2025, TradeWave's research found:

- The strongest long-term stretch ran from about **October 26 to September 3**.
- The weakest stretch, **September 4 to October 25** (about 52 days), averaged about **-1%**.
- In a hypothetical $10,000 compounding illustration that ignores taxes and costs, sitting out only those 52 days each year grew to about **$17.7 million**, versus about **$3.9 million** for holding all year.

Individual stocks show the same shape even more strongly. For many large companies the best long window runs from roughly late September or October into the following summer. Every security has its own calendar, though, so test each holding rather than applying one date to all of them.

The [election cycle](/learn/presidential-election-cycle-regimes.html) adds another layer. In midterm years the weak stretch has historically been deeper and the recovery window stronger, which is the basis of [the 100-Year Pattern](/100-year-pattern).

## Ways to Use a Weak Window Without Selling

Selling a long-term holding every September can trigger taxes and transaction costs that eat the benefit. Common alternatives:

- **Time new money.** Add to positions after the weak stretch rather than before it.
- **Time rebalancing.** Trim winners going into a historically weak window and add back after it.
- **Write covered calls.** Collect option premium on shares you already own during a historically flat or weak stretch.
- **Hedge instead of exiting.** Use a protective position during the weak window and keep the core holding.
- **Use tax-advantaged accounts.** Seasonal in-and-out timing is simplest where trades do not create taxable gains.

## Cautions

- Compounding illustrations are hypothetical and exclude taxes, fees and slippage.
- A long window can have deep drawdowns inside it even when it finishes higher. A perfect win rate does not mean a smooth ride.
- Diversify across holdings, sectors and asset classes so one calendar is never your whole plan.
