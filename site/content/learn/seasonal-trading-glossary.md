## Patterns and Windows

**Seasonal pattern.** A recurring tendency for a market to move one way around the same calendar dates, usually driven by a real-world cycle. See [What Is a Seasonal Pattern?](/learn/what-is-a-seasonal-pattern.html)

**Seasonal window.** A start date plus a holding period: buy (or sell short) here, hold this many days, then exit.

**Trading wave.** TradeWave's name for a seasonal window with a measured track record.

**The five parts of a pattern.** Symbol, start date, days held, year set and direction. Change any one and it is a different pattern.

**Calendar days.** TradeWave windows are counted in calendar days, with the entry day as day 1. A 31-day window starting July 1 ends July 31.

**Direction (Long / Short).** Long expects a rise; short expects a fall. For a short pattern, a falling year counts as a win.

**Pivot zone.** The point in a window where the typical path bends and the real move begins.

**Peak zone.** The natural high point of a seasonal move. Holding past it tends to give gains back. See [When to Exit a Seasonal Trade](/learn/when-to-exit-a-seasonal-trade.html)

**Window alignment.** Small, honest shifts of a window's edges to match the pivot or peak zone. Legitimate, unlike curve-fitting.

**Curve-fitting (overfitting).** Searching through many date ranges or settings until the backtest looks perfect, which captures noise instead of a real effect. See [Curve-Fitting vs Window Alignment](/learn/curve-fitting-vs-window-alignment.html)

**Reverse Date Range.** A TradeWave tool that flips a window to its complement, such as the weak stretch of the year to the strong remainder.

**Best Seasonal Hold Window.** The long holding period, typically five months or more, that historically produced the best cumulative result for a security. See [Seasonal Investing](/learn/seasonal-investing-for-long-term-investors.html)

## Years and Regimes

**Year set (lookback).** The exact historical years used to measure a pattern, such as the last 10 consecutive years or the last 8 midterm years.

**Consecutive mode.** Measuring a pattern over the last N calendar years in a row.

**Presidential election cycle (PE cycle).** The four-year U.S. cycle used as a lens: PE (election year), PE+1 (post-election), PE+2 (midterm) and PE+3 (pre-election). See [The Presidential Election Cycle Lens](/learn/presidential-election-cycle-regimes.html)

**PE+2 (midterm year).** The second year after a presidential election. Historically the most volatile phase of the cycle. 2026 is a PE+2 year.

**Regime.** The lens used to measure a pattern, consecutive years or one cycle phase. A regime is not what the market is; it is how you choose to look at it.

**Regime flip.** When changing only the lens turns a scan from mostly bullish to mostly bearish, or the reverse, for the same dates.

**Dominant regime.** The lens in which a market's behavior is clearer and more consistent.

**"10 of 10".** Ten qualifying years in the chosen year set. In cycle mode this means ten phase years, never ten years in a row.

## Performance Metrics

**Win rate (percent profitable).** The share of sampled years a window finished in the right direction. Best read as a count, such as 9 of 10.

**Average gain (AvgP).** The mean return of the window across sampled years. Can be distorted by outliers.

**Median gain.** The middle year's return after sorting. Shows a typical year. See [Median vs Average Return](/learn/median-vs-average-return.html)

**Hero year.** A single outsized year that carries a pattern's average.

**Standard deviation.** How spread out the yearly results are. Lower means steadier.

**Sharpe Ratio (SR).** Return relative to volatility. Higher means more consistent gains. See [Sharpe Ratio Explained](/learn/sharpe-ratio-explained.html)

**MFE (Maximum Favorable Excursion).** How far a trade moved in your favor at its best point inside the window. Used for targets.

**MAE (Maximum Adverse Excursion).** How far a trade moved against you at its worst point. Used for stops and sizing. See [MFE and MAE Explained](/learn/mfe-and-mae-explained.html)

**TWA (TradeWave Average).** An average-outcome measure built from MFE, reflecting favorable movement during the window.

**TWR (TradeWave Ratio).** TradeWave's signature quality score: Sharpe-style, using MFE. Common screens are 1.5 for consecutive studies and 1.0 for cycle studies. See [TradeWave Ratio and TradeWave Average](/learn/tradewave-ratio-twr-and-twa.html)

**Cumulative return.** The compounded result of trading the window every year in the sample.

**Buy and hold (B&H).** The benchmark: owning from January 1 to January 1 every year. A seasonal window earns its place only if it beats buy and hold.

**Trend Long / Trend Short.** Quick readouts of how often the broader trend favored long or short positions around the window.

## Trading Math and Risk

**Expectancy.** Average profit or loss per trade: (win rate x average win) - (loss rate x average loss). See [Trading Expectancy Explained](/learn/trading-expectancy-explained.html)

**R-multiple.** A result expressed as a multiple of the amount risked. Risk $500 and make $1,500 and the trade is +3R.

**Risk reward ratio.** The size of the typical win relative to the typical loss. See [Win Rate vs Risk Reward Ratio](/learn/win-rate-vs-risk-reward-ratio.html)

**Position sizing.** Choosing how much to buy so that hitting the stop costs a planned amount. Position size = risk per trade / stop distance.

**1% rule.** Risking about 1% of the account per trade. See [Position Sizing and Stop Losses](/learn/position-sizing-and-stop-loss-seasonal-trades.html)

**Stop loss.** The price that says the trade is wrong and triggers an exit.

**Time stop.** Exiting on a planned date regardless of price. For seasonal trades, usually the window's end date.

## Market Seasonality Terms

**Best Six Months.** The observation, popularized by the Stock Trader's Almanac, that the stock market historically did most of its work from about November through April.

**Sell in May and Go Away.** The folk version of Best Six Months. TradeWave's research finds the May to October weakness has concentrated in midterm years.

**The Lost Summer.** TradeWave's name for the weakest stretch of the year for the S&P 500, about September 4 to October 25, which averaged a small loss across nearly a century of data.

**The 100-Year Pattern.** A midterm-year S&P 500 window from about September 27 to about July 18 of the following year that rose in 23 of 24 cycles since 1930. See [the 100-Year Pattern](/100-year-pattern).
