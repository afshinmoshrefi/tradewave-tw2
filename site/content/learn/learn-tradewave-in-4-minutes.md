- Stocks have seasons. You cannot see them on an ordinary price chart, but they show up when you line the same calendar window up across many years.
- TradeWave has three parts: a list of opportunities, a lab for checking the evidence, and a portfolio for saving and tracking patterns.
- The opportunity list starts pre-filtered to windows that worked in at least 9 of the last 10 years.
- Four numbers tell most of the story: how often it worked, how much it typically made, how bumpy the ride was, and how it compared with simply holding the market.

## Stocks Have Seasons

Retailers ring up holiday sales in the fourth quarter. Refiners prepare for the summer driving season. Companies set budgets on fiscal calendars, and funds rebalance at quarter end. These recurring behaviors leave footprints in prices that tend to show up around the same dates year after year.

A single price chart hides those footprints because every year looks different. Stack the same window, say February 5 to March 17, across ten or twenty years, and the rhythm becomes visible. That is what TradeWave does at scale. If you are new to the idea, [What Is Seasonal Trading?](/learn/what-is-seasonal-trading.html) covers it in more depth.

## The Three Parts of TradeWave

1. **The Opportunities Table** scans thousands of securities and calendar windows and ranks the setups with the strongest historical record for a given start date. See [Finding High-Probability Trading Waves](/learn/finding-high-probability-trading-waves.html).
2. **The Wave Viewer** is the lab. It loads one pattern and shows the evidence year by year in the Profit Bar Chart, the Trend Chart and the Stats Table. See [Using the Wave Viewer](/learn/using-the-wave-viewer.html).
3. **The Portfolio Manager** saves the exact configuration of each pattern so you can recall it, track it and set reminders. See [Using the Portfolio Manager](/learn/using-the-portfolio-manager.html).

## Step 1: Start With the Filtered List

The list opens already filtered to windows that finished in the right direction in at least nine of the last ten years. That filter removes most of the noise before you look at anything. Sort by the Sharpe Ratio to bring the steadiest records to the top.

## Step 2: Read the Sharpe Ratio in Plain English

The Sharpe Ratio compares a window's typical gain with how much the results bounced around from year to year. A high Sharpe Ratio means the gains were both meaningful and consistent. A low one means the average may be propped up by one or two big years. The full explanation is in [Sharpe Ratio Explained for Seasonal Traders](/learn/sharpe-ratio-explained.html).

## Step 3: Read the Bars, One Bar Per Year

Click a row to open it in the Wave Viewer. The Profit Bar Chart draws one bar for each historical year. You want to see mostly bars in the right direction, losing years that stayed small, and no single giant bar carrying the whole result.

## Step 4: Check the Four Numbers That Matter

- **Percent profitable:** how many of the sampled years finished in the right direction, such as 9 of 10.
- **Average and median gain:** the typical size of the move. When the median is far below the average, a hero year is doing the heavy lifting. See [Median vs Average Return](/learn/median-vs-average-return.html).
- **Sharpe Ratio:** how steady those gains were.
- **Cumulative return versus buy and hold:** whether trading only this window beat simply owning the market.

## Step 5: Look at the AI Score and the Price Chart

The AI score is a second opinion on how stable the historical record looks. The price chart shows the current year against the historical projections, so you can see whether this year is tracking the usual path. Treat both as context. TradeWave generates the statistics; the AI reads them and never invents them.

## Step 6: Set the Reminder

One click saves the start and end dates to your calendar. Most seasonal edges fail in execution, not in research: the date passes, or the exit gets improvised. A reminder fixes both.

## The Five Steps in One Line

Filter to 9 of 10 years, sort by Sharpe, read the bars, check the four numbers, set the reminder.
